## Comparative Analysis
1. **Cost** – The Mustang Mach‑E offers a lower entry price (≈$15k) and a more aggressive range for its price point. Tesla’s higher price is offset by its larger battery and longer range.
2. **Charging** – With a 150 kW charger, the Mach‑E can reach 80 % in ~25 min, faster than the Model Y’s 35 min. However, Tesla’s Supercharger network is more extensive, reducing range anxiety for long‑haul routes.
3. **Range & Payload** – The Mach‑E’s 300 mi range is close to the Model Y’s 270 mi, but the Mustang’s cargo space is significantly smaller, which may limit sales‑support duties that require carrying tools or samples.
4. **Brand & Perception** – Tesla’s brand aligns with tech‑savvy sales teams, while the Mustang offers a sporty image that may resonate with certain customer segments.
5. **Maintenance & Reliability** – Historical data shows Tesla’s service network is growing but still limited in some regions; Ford has a broader dealer network which could simplify maintenance for a 100‑car fleet.

## Recommendation
Given the 100‑car fleet goal, **Ford Mustang Mach‑E** is the preferable choice. It delivers competitive range, faster charging, and a lower upfront cost while leveraging Ford’s extensive dealer infrastructure for maintenance. The Model Y’s higher price and slightly larger cargo capacity are outweighed by the Mach‑E’s operational advantages for a sales fleet.
